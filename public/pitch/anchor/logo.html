The idea

An anchor holds. The heart is why.

The one change we make to the anchor is the ring at the top, the part the rope passes through. That becomes a heart. It is not decoration stuck on the side. It is the piece that connects the anchor to everything else, which is a fair description of what the giving program is to your business.

Color

Four colors, and each one has to come from somewhere real.

This is the part we need you for. Every small business identity that got taken seriously in the last two years pulled its colors off a photograph of something that actually exists, and then said so out loud. We do not want to specify "navy." We want to specify a navy from a thing in your town.

Anchor Navy#14293C Carries the trust. Text, the mark, dark surfaces.
Cause Red#C4362B The giving, and allowed to fill whole sections rather than trim.
Sand#E4D7C3 The third voice. Keeps it off the navy-and-white default.
Paper#F7F3EC The page. Warm, not white.

What we need from you

A photograph of something in Manchester that means something to you. A brick wall, the water, a sign, the building you work in. We pull the navy and the red out of it, and the guidelines say where they came from. It costs nothing and it turns a palette into a decision.

And the thing that matters more than any of it

Nothing about this mark is specific to you yet. It could be any agency with an anchor in the name. Every small business identity worth copying in the last two years earned it by naming the place, the year, or the family. If there is a founding year, a street, or a name that belongs in this, tell us and we will build it in.
