The refinement

Same logo. Cleaner cut.

Nothing about the idea changed: a navy anchor holding, gold water moving over it. What changed is the drawing. The linework is regularized so it survives every size, the flukes come to real points, the stock keeps its knobs, and the wave is rebuilt as three ribbons, because three ribbons can move.

Color

The palette comes out of the logo, and it is measured.

Your logo already answers the color question: navy and gold. What we added is the arithmetic. Every pairing on the site is computed for contrast, which is why the gold has a darker cut for text and why gold surfaces always carry navy lettering rather than white.

Anchor Navy#14293C Carries the trust. Text, the mark, buttons, dark surfaces.
Anchor Gold#C19A49 The water. Marks the giving, and always carries navy lettering.
Sand#E4D7C3 The third voice. Keeps it off the navy-and-white default.
Paper#F7F3EC The page. Warm, not white.

Why the gold has more than one value

The gold in your logo is beautiful and it is quiet: small text set in it fails every legibility standard there is. So it works the way a real brand system works: the logo gold for fills and the wave, a darker cut for text on light pages, a lighter cut for text on navy. Same family, measured for each job.

And the thing that matters more than any of it

Every small business identity worth copying in the last two years earned it by naming the place, the year, or the family. If there is a founding year, a street, or a name that belongs in this mark, tell us and we will build it in.
